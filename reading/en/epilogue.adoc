== The Person Who Learned to Draw Maps

[quote]
____

=== The Story

TeamOrder's notification feature moved beyond the demo and made it into production.

Three months in, the expected happened. Two team leads asked to reduce their notification settings — too many were coming through. The "notification fatigue" failure scenario that had appeared in the Pre-mortem had become real. Because the team had anticipated it, they added priority filtering in one week.

Six months in, users had grown to 150. It was time to pull out the scalability design that had been set aside in Session 7 as "low risk for now." Because the team had expected this too, they scheduled a review without alarm.

One day Jiyeon posted on Slack:

=== Closing — To the Reader

This workshop did not teach technology.

FAC, Context-Free Questions, Black Box, Domain Model, Spike, analogical reasoning, Risk-Driven — these are all tools. Tools eventually become less useful. Better tools come along.

But what lies behind the tools remains.

The willingness to understand problems first. The patience to explore ambiguity rather than be unsettled by it. The ability to identify what not to build. And — the belief that moving in the right direction is ultimately faster than moving quickly.

This does not emerge automatically from three or four years of experience. It requires deliberate practice. This workshop was the beginning of that practice.

'''

=== What Was Learned in This Workshop

A final review of the tools and concepts covered across eight sessions.

_Problem Space Exploration_

[cols="1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Session | Tool | Core Question
| 1 | Einstellung Effect, Presumptive Architecture awareness | Am I understanding the problem, or recognizing a pattern?
| 2 | FAC (Functions / Attributes / Constraints) | Have I uncovered all three layers of the requirement?
| 3 | Context-Free Questions, Black Box Test | Did I explore without presupposing a solution?
| 4 | Symptom-Problem-Root Cause layers, Domain Model | Did I consciously choose which layer to solve?
|===


_Handling Ambiguity_

[cols="1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Session | Tool | Core Question
| 5 | Resolvable vs Essential diagnosis, Spike | Is this ambiguity resolvable through asking, or only knowable through doing?
|===


_Solution Space Exploration_

[cols="1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Session | Tool | Core Question
| 6 | Analogical reasoning, problem-type reduction, Trade-off research | Have I seen this structure somewhere before?
| 7 | Engineering Risk identification, Quality Attributes, Risk-Driven design | Where should I focus my design energy?
| 8 | Problem-Solution Fit, Pre-mortem | Is the problem I defined actually connected to the solution I built?
|===


'''

=== So What Changes?

Before this workshop:

----
Requirement received
    ↓ (15 seconds)
Enter Solution Space
    ↓
Design + Build
    ↓
"This isn't what I asked for"
    ↓
Rework
----

After this workshop:

----
Requirement received
    ↓
"What do I know right now?"
    ↓
Problem Space exploration (FAC, Context-Free Q, Domain Model...)
    ↓
Identify core + what can wait + what doesn't need to be built
    ↓
Build with focus
    ↓
Early delivery → Feedback → Validated expansion
----

Reducing rework is not the goal. Reducing rework is a byproduct. The goal — is getting users what they need, sooner.

'''

If Seunghyun's learning could be written in a single line:

_"Spending more time on the problem is how you build the solution faster."_

If that line comes to mind the next time you receive a requirement — this workshop succeeded.
